# Zion Tech Hub: Ad Budget Allocation and Growth Recommendations

## Ad Budget Allocation

Assuming a first ad budget with no prior history to lean on, here is how I'd split it, and why.

| Channel | Budget Share | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| X (Twitter) | 55% | The only channel showing genuine, spike-adjusted organic growth (39.6% to 62.8% across cohorts). Also the strongest channel for international reach (72.9% to 86.2% of international registrants used it). Broad audience, not tied to one profession, so ad creative can stay general rather than niche. |
| LinkedIn | 30% | The most stable, predictable channel across both cohorts (24.8% to 23.3%, barely moved). Disproportionately strong for Healthcare Data Analytics registrants. If the goal is reliability rather than reach, this is where it comes from. |
| Test budget (Facebook/Instagram reactivation) | 10% | Both channels were present at low levels in Cohort 9 and vanished in Cohort 10. Rather than writing them off completely on two data points, a small test budget answers the question cheaply: was this a real decline, or did Zion Tech Hub simply stop posting there? |
| Referral incentive | 5% | Referral has stayed small but present in both cohorts (2.4% each), which suggests some natural word of mouth already exists. This isn't ad spend in the traditional sense, it's a small incentive budget (a discount, a bonus, a shoutout) to actively encourage the referrals that are already happening quietly. |

**What I'd deliberately not do with a first budget:** put money behind WhatsApp. It's the fastest declining channel in the data (28.9% to 7.0%, spike-adjusted) and reaches almost no one outside Nigeria. That doesn't mean abandon WhatsApp altogether, it likely still matters as a community and support tool, just not as a channel worth paying to acquire new registrants through right now.

**On targeting:** X (Twitter)'s occupation spread is broad, not concentrated in one profession, so ad copy here should speak to career changers and skill builders generally, not one job title. LinkedIn's audience already leans Healthcare, so ad creative and course promotion there should lean into that strength rather than trying to force a generic message. For international reach specifically, South Africa, Ghana, Kenya, and Uganda are the four markets already showing consistent organic interest across two cohorts, so any international ad targeting should start there rather than spreading thin across many countries with no track record.

## Growth Recommendations for Cohort 11

**1. Make course selection clearer before someone commits, to cut down on registration switching.**
Nearly two thirds of people who registered more than once (24 of 37) chose a different course the second time. That's a real signal that people aren't confident in their first choice. A short "which course fits you" guide, or clearer course descriptions on the registration form itself, could reduce this back and forth and the extra admin it creates on your end.

**2. Investigate why WhatsApp is declining before deciding what to do about it.**
A drop from 28.9% to 7.0% is too steep to ignore, but the data can't tell us why on its own; it could be community fatigue, a change in how actively it's being promoted, or something else entirely. This is worth a direct conversation with whoever manages that channel before either doubling down or walking away from it.

**3. Plan a deliberate, timed push near the end of each cohort's registration window, not just an always-on campaign.**
Both cohorts show the same shape: a huge concentration of registrations in a short window close to the deadline (42% of Cohort 9 in a 2 day window 11 days before close; 74% of Cohort 10 in a single Thursday/Friday). Whether or not this was planned the first time, it clearly works. Building a deliberate "final week" push into the Cohort 11 plan, rather than treating steady spend as the whole strategy, could replicate this intentionally instead of hoping it happens again.

**4. Build a real referral program around your most consistent channel's audience.**
LinkedIn's stability suggests a base of people who trust this program enough to keep coming back to it, cohort after cohort. Referral registrations are already happening organically at a low but steady rate. A structured incentive (even a small one) aimed at past registrants and their networks could turn a passive pattern into an active one.

**5. Look into the Healthcare Data Analytics decline before assuming it will fix itself.**
This course dropped from 24.6% to 17.9% of registrations, a real decline even after accounting for Cohort 10's expanded course catalog. Since LinkedIn (your steadiest channel) is also your strongest source of Healthcare-interested registrants, a continued decline here could eventually mean LinkedIn's registrations shrink too, even if LinkedIn itself isn't the problem. Worth understanding whether this is about the course content, its positioning, or market saturation.

**6. Treat international growth as a X (Twitter) and LinkedIn play, not a WhatsApp one.**
Nigeria, South Africa, Ghana, and Kenya are consistently your top four countries across two cohorts. International registrants use X (Twitter) and LinkedIn at meaningfully higher rates than Nigerian registrants do, and barely touch WhatsApp. If international growth is a genuine goal for Cohort 11, the channels to lean into for that specifically are already visible in the data.
